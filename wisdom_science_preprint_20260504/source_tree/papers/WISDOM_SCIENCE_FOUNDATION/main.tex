\begin{abstract}
AI evaluation is dominated by first-attempt performance: accuracy, pass rate, success rate, and benchmark rank.
Deployment, however, depends on a different property: whether a system becomes better, more stable, and more transferable after failure, feedback, perturbation, and repeated experience.
We call this research program \emph{Wisdom Science}.
It separates first-round intelligence from longitudinal wisdom, introduces Wisdom Quotient (WQ) and Embodied Wisdom Quotient (EWQ), studies architecture-dependent wisdom scaling, and requires evidence gates for empirical claims.
This paper consolidates the current Wisdom Science package: SOVEREIGN, Cognitive Immunity, WisdomBench, the Intelligence-Wisdom Gap, the Second Scaling Law, WisdomBench-Embodied, Representation Genesis, Embodied Failure Immunity, the Wisdom Science Formal Core, and the Physics/Engineering Core.
We provide reproducible artifacts including a claim registry, a terminology registry, a Zenodo bibliography, a macro-mining pass over 130 files, a representation-compression study, formal sanity checks, physics/engineering checks, and an evidence index.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Shift}

The dominant question in AI evaluation is: how well does the system perform on the first try?
This question is necessary but incomplete.
A system deployed in the real world encounters repeated tasks, changing environments, failures, adversarial pressure, and opportunities for transfer.
The relevant question becomes:

\begin{quote}
After experience, does the system become better, more stable, and more transferable?
\end{quote}

Wisdom Science is the study of this second question.
It does not replace intelligence evaluation.
It adds a longitudinal axis.

\section{Core Definitions}

\begin{definition}[First-round Intelligence]
First-round intelligence $I$ is the performance of an agent before task-specific feedback from the current evaluation sequence.
For embodied agents we write $I_E$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Wisdom]
Wisdom is observable improvement conditioned on repeated experience, feedback, failure, and transfer opportunities.
It is not a claim about consciousness, virtue, or alignment.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Wisdom Quotient]
For $N$ tasks and $R$ rounds, define
\[
\mathrm{WQ}
= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i,\quad
w_i =
\begin{cases}
\frac{s_i^{(R)}-s_i^{(1)}}{s_{\max}-s_i^{(1)}} & s_i^{(1)}<s_{\max}\\
0 & s_i^{(1)}=s_{\max}.
\end{cases}
\]
WQ measures normalized cross-round learning gain.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Embodied Wisdom Quotient]
For $K$ embodied tasks, define
\[
\mathrm{EWQ}(M)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{t=1}^{K}\left[s_R(t)-s_1(t)\right].
\]
EWQ measures auditable improvement across repeated physical or simulated embodied trials.
\end{definition}

\section{Formal Guardrails}

The Formal Core separates definitions, theorems, hypotheses, and empirical claims.
It proves four guardrails that the rest of the package must obey.
First, under non-regression and bounded scores, WQ lies in $[0,1]$.
Second, first-round intelligence and wisdom can rank systems differently, so a first-attempt leaderboard cannot determine a wisdom leaderboard.
Third, positive WQ or EWQ does not by itself identify a learning mechanism: the same score trace can be produced by genuine feedback use or by hidden round scheduling.
Fourth, aggregate wisdom rankings can reverse if task mixtures are not fixed, so task weights and strata must be reported.

These results are intentionally restrictive.
They make the framework harder to overclaim and easier to defend.

\section{Physics and Engineering Grounding}

The Physics/Engineering Core turns the main variables into operational quantities.
Plasticity is treated as normalized response to useful experience.
Homeostasis is inverse perturbation gain.
Failure immunity is repeat-failure attenuation with related and unrelated controls.
Wisdom efficiency is gain per compute, wall-time, API, storage, or robot-wear cost.

The engineering rule is simple: a result is not strong evidence unless it passes metric, stability, provenance, cost, and duplicate-cell gates.
This prevents physics language from becoming metaphor and prevents engineering language from becoming presentation polish.

\section{The Second Scaling Law}

The central hypothesis is that wisdom scales with architectural qualities and experience:
\[
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{T}}[W(M)]
= C_{\mathcal{T}}\,
\Phi(\mathcal{A})^{\alpha}
\Psi(\mathcal{A})^{\beta}
H(\mathcal{A})^{\gamma}
|\mathcal{E}|^{\delta}.
\]

Here $\Phi$ is plasticity, $\Psi$ is transferable failure immunity, $H$ is homeostasis, and $|\mathcal{E}|$ is the amount of experience.
The embodied analogue is
\[
W_E \propto \Phi_E^\alpha\Psi_E^\beta H_E^\gamma.
\]

\begin{remark}
This law is a testable framework, not a finished universal exponent theorem.
Current evidence supports the separation between first-round competence and longitudinal improvement, while larger architecture sweeps are required to estimate universal exponents.
\end{remark}

\section{Evidence Discipline}

Wisdom claims are easy to overstate.
For that reason, the package uses claim registries and evidence gates.
A claim should name its evidence source, strength, and limitation.
For embodied evaluation, every result should point to raw logs, trajectory records, simulator metadata, checkpoint provenance, generated tables, and scripts.
For text-agent evaluation, the six-model API panel records raw responses, prompt/response hashes, judge identity, fixed task IDs, seeds, rounds, and summary tables without storing credentials.
Its current pilot strengthens cross-model coverage, but it cannot be used as robot rollout or public VLA checkpoint evidence.

\section{Embodied Failure Immunity}

Embodied Failure Immunity applies Cognitive Immunity to robot failures.
The key object is an embodied antigen: a compact, evidence-backed description of a failure event, causal cue, recovery opportunity, transfer group, and provenance.
The first atlas schema includes failure classes such as grasp/contact failure, spatial placement failure, visual grounding failure, instruction grounding failure, perturbation fragility, recovery-loop failure, and simulator/provenance failure.

\section{Views and Organisms}

Two additional principles complete the current stack.
First, \emph{the view is not the world}: a single observation may correspond to many latent states, meanings, or strategic intentions.
Perspectival Grounding therefore requires agents to preserve competing hypotheses and select additional views, actions, context, or belief-model evidence before collapsing ambiguity.
Second, \emph{the brain is not the organism}: embodied wisdom depends on policy, senses, routing, metabolic budget, joints, immune memory, calibration, social norms, and counter-reflexive game cognition.
Whole-Organism Intelligence turns those organ-like subsystems into engineering gates rather than biological metaphors.

\section{Leaderboard Schema}

The proposed leaderboard separates first-round intelligence from longitudinal wisdom:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Language agents: report $I$, WQ, $\Phi$, $\Psi$, $H$, generalization, and repeat failure.
  \item Embodied agents: report $I_E$, EWQ, $\Phi_E$, $\Psi_E$, $H_E$, trajectories, and provenance.
  \item Representation systems: report compression gain, unpacking cost, transfer, reuse, novelty, and residual absorption.
\end{itemize}

This schema prevents a common leaderboard error: treating a high first attempt as evidence of learning.

\section{Limitations}

Wisdom Science is not a claim that benchmark improvement equals moral wisdom.
WQ and EWQ are operational metrics.
Positive WQ or EWQ may arise from stochasticity, hidden state, task ordering, or simulator artifacts.
Therefore, mechanism-level provenance is required.
The Second Scaling Law remains a framework with preliminary evidence, not a universal exponent theorem.
Representation compression is a useful signal but not truth.

\section{Conclusion}

Wisdom Science adds a second axis to AI evaluation.
The first axis asks how competent a system is before experience.
The second asks whether it becomes better after experience.
The resulting research program connects benchmarks, architecture, failure learning, embodied evaluation, representation search, and evidence discipline.
The goal is not merely to build larger models, but to build systems that can fail, learn, transfer, stabilize, and improve.
